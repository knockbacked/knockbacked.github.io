📖 Overview

The manufacturing-forecasting-framework is a downloadable Windows application built for people who need reliable monthly demand forecasts without wrestling with code, spreadsheets, or confusing statistical tools. It uses a proven machine learning method called Random Forest, which works well with manufacturing data such as sales history, production volumes, and seasonal patterns.

What makes this application different is its focus on transparency and reproducibility. Every forecast comes with a chronological backtest — a way to check how well the model would have predicted past months — and a comparison against simple, transparent baselines. That means you can see not just the forecast, but also whether the forecast is actually better than a naive guess. The guided local app walks you through each step, from loading your data to reading the results, so you always know what is happening and why.

Whether you are a production planner, a supply chain analyst, or a small business owner, this framework helps you turn historical monthly demand into a clear, defensible forecast you can act on.

🧭 How the Forecast Works

You do not need to understand the inner workings to use the application, but knowing the three main ideas will help you interpret the results with confidence.

Random Forest model

The Random Forest is a collection of many simple decision trees that each look at your demand history in a slightly different way. Their answers are combined into a single forecast. This approach is popular in manufacturing because it copes well with irregular patterns, promotions, and seasonal swings without requiring you to specify formulas by hand.

Chronological backtests

A backtest is a practice run. The application hides the most recent months of your data, trains the model on the earlier months, and then asks it to predict the hidden months. Because the test always moves forward in time, just like real life, the resulting accuracy figures are a fair reflection of how the model is likely to perform on future months.

Transparent baselines

A baseline is a deliberately simple forecast used as a yardstick. For example, one baseline simply repeats last month’s demand, and another repeats the same month from the previous year. If the Random Forest cannot beat these simple rules, the app tells you clearly. That honesty helps you decide whether to trust the model or fall back on a straightforward approach.

🔁 Reproducibility and Trust

Reproducibility means that a forecast can be repeated exactly. The framework records the settings used, the order of the data, and the random seeds that drive the model. Run the same data again and you get the same numbers, which is essential when forecasts are reviewed, audited, or handed over to another team. You can keep a record of each run and compare it with later versions of your data to see how your forecast has evolved.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know how to program?

No. The application is a guided tool with plain-language screens. If you can open a file and click a button, you can use it.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The app runs entirely on your own computer. Your demand data never leaves your machine.

What file format should my data be in?

A CSV spreadsheet is the most common choice. You can export this from Excel by choosing “Save As” and selecting CSV. The guided app explains exactly which columns it needs before you load anything.

How long does a forecast take?

For typical monthly manufacturing histories, a full run with backtests usually finishes in a few minutes on an ordinary Windows computer. Larger datasets take a little longer, and the app shows progress while it works.

Can I compare two versions of my data?

Yes. Because results are reproducible, you can run the app on an older snapshot and a newer snapshot and compare the outputs side by side to understand how your forecast has changed.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

  • The download does not start: Refresh the page and click the download button again. If your browser blocks the download, look for a small banner near the address bar and choose to keep the file.
  • The app will not open: Right-click the downloaded file and choose Run as administrator. Also check that the download finished completely before opening it.
  • My data is not accepted: Open your file in Excel and confirm it has one row per month with a date column and a demand column. Re-save it as CSV and try again.
  • The results look unusual: Check the backtest and baseline comparison shown by the app. If the baselines perform better, your history may be too short or too irregular for the model to add value yet.
